Story

Onimusha: Way of the Sword is Capcom's 2026 revival of the Onimusha franchise, the studio's foundational feudal-Japan action-adventure series that ran from 2001 through 2006 across four mainline entries. The franchise's signature premise is straightforward and durable: a lone swordsman inherits the Oni Gauntlet, a demon-forged weapon that absorbs the souls of slain enemies, and uses it to fight back an invasion of shape-shifting demons across a stylized feudal Japan. The original entries starred figures drawn from Japanese historical tradition — Samanosuke Akechi, Jubei Yagyu, Soki — refashioned as demon hunters carrying the weight of their historical eras' actual political conflicts against a coordinated supernatural threat.

The revival's protagonist is Miyamoto Musashi, arguably the most famous swordsman in Japanese history and the author of The Book of Five Rings, the martial-philosophical text that has shaped Japanese sword tradition since its 1645 composition. The historical Musashi lived from 1584 to 1645, fought his first duel at thirteen, undertook the musha shugyo warrior pilgrimage across his early adulthood, and is credited with sixty-one undefeated duels across his lifetime. Capcom's campaign is set in the early Edo period — the years immediately following the 1603 establishment of the Tokugawa shogunate — and dwells on the young Musashi's formative years, the period during which he developed the two-sword Niten Ichi-ryu style that would define his tradition.

The campaign frames Musashi's warrior pilgrimage against the franchise's signature demonic-invasion premise. The Genma — the franchise's shape-shifting demon faction — have infiltrated the political vacuum of the post-Sengoku transition, exploiting the collapse of the daimyo warfare system to establish operational footholds across Japan's temples, castles, and mountain passes. Musashi's musha shugyo pulls him repeatedly into contact with Genma operations, and the campaign frames his acquisition of the Oni Gauntlet as the historical moment his swordsmanship crossed from technical mastery into legend.

Capcom's revival rebuilds the franchise's core loop with modern technical fidelity and the studio's most cinematic direction to date. The Oni Gauntlet's soul-absorbing combat is preserved as the campaign's central mechanical identity — every slain Genma yields a soul that Musashi can channel into weapon upgrades, magical abilities, and the campaign's signature transformation sequences. The exploration structure follows the franchise's traditional deliberate-progression design, with each region requiring the acquisition of specific soul-based abilities to unlock its subsequent zones. The boss encounters are designed as sustained combat set-pieces staged at the campaign's most acclaimed environmental locations, with the franchise's signature enemy design refined for the modern console generation.

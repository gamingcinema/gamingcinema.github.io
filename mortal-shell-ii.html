Story

Mortal Shell II is Cold Symmetry's 2026 follow-up to its 2020 breakout Mortal Shell, published by Playstack for PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S and PC. It is a standalone sequel — the studio states that newcomers can start here — and it widens the original's compact, tightly authored world into an interconnected open map of ravaged regions, hidden structures and landmarks, with more than sixty dungeons to uncover. The tone remains what the franchise has always been: bleak, ritualistic dark fantasy, where every statue, ruin and corpse feels like a fragment of a civilization that has already ended.

You play the Harbinger, a being prophesied to reclaim the Ova — the unborn eggs of the blessed Undermether — which have been stolen and are now ruthlessly guarded by grotesque creatures. The campaign frames that quest as a rebellion against the false gods who rule the shattered land, and the story is delivered the way the series prefers: sparingly, through environments, recovered memories and the lingering will of the dead rather than lengthy exposition.

The signature mechanic is Shell possession. Scattered across the world are the dormant forms of forgotten warriors; once found, each can be awakened and inhabited, and the Harbinger gains that warrior's innate strengths and abilities. Cold Symmetry lists eight playable Shells, each with its own combat style, and the connection with a Shell deepens over time, unlocking new talents, upgrades and memories that reveal its past. Combat is faster and less forgiving than in the original, built around deeper weapon design with extensive upgrade options and an emphasis on free exploration.

The original Mortal Shell, released in 2020, built its identity on a handful of ideas: a lean, interlinked world called Fallgrim, a roster of Shells rather than conventional character classes, and the ability to harden — briefly turning to stone in the middle of a fight to survive a blow. Cold Symmetry describes the sequel as significantly expanding on that foundation, with faster and unforgiving combat, deeper weapon design with extensive upgrade options, and an emphasis on free exploration. The result keeps the demanding, ritual feel of its predecessor while offering far more room to decide where to go next and how to fight when you get there.

The walkthrough captures the complete campaign in continuous cinematic form. For viewers new to the series: the appeal of Mortal Shell is the tension between patience and aggression — learning an enemy, choosing the right Shell for it, and committing to the strike. Mortal Shell II keeps that loop and gives it a far larger world to play out in.

Story

Clive Barker's Hellraiser: Revival is a single-player, first-person survival horror action game released on October 8, 2026 for PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S and PC. It was developed by Boss Team Games and published by Saber Interactive, the studio behind World War Z and Warhammer 40,000: Space Marine 2. Franchise creator Clive Barker was consulted on the story from the early days of development, and Doug Bradley, who played Pinhead in the first eight Hellraiser films, returns to voice the character.

You play Aidan, whose girlfriend Sunny has been taken by Pinhead and the Cenobites. In an all-new story set in the Hellraiser universe, Aidan unwittingly kickstarts Pinhead's ritual to unleash the ultimate torment on the world, and descends into the otherworldly hellscape of the Labyrinth to bring her back. Everything he does from that point on is shaped by the strange puzzle box he carries.

That box is the Genesis Configuration, and its supernatural powers sit alongside conventional melee and ranged weapons. Combat is up close and decidedly gory, pitting Aidan against Hell's army of devians, cultists and priests, while puzzle-solving and exploration carry him through the vast, multi-level maze of the Labyrinth as he tries to stop the Cenobites from carrying out their plan. The game is openly built for mature audiences: it earned its ESRB rating without cuts, and the team pushes violence, sexual content and adult themes as far as the franchise's source material suggests.

The Hellraiser mythology began with Clive Barker's 1986 novella The Hellbound Heart, which Barker adapted and directed as the 1987 film Hellraiser. Its central object, the puzzle box that summons the Cenobites — the extra-dimensional beings led by the figure fans call Pinhead — became one of horror's most recognizable props, and Revival builds its story around a new box, the Genesis Configuration. The franchise has always treated the Cenobites as explorers of experience at its extremes rather than simple demons, and that tone carries into the game's themes of desire, sacrifice and consequence.

For viewers new to the franchise: Revival is a standalone story, and its appeal lies in the Hellraiser mythology — pleasure and pain as two sides of the same door, and a box that opens onto something far worse than the person who opened it ever expected.

Story

Ghost of Tsushima is Sucker Punch Productions' 2020 PS4 open-world samurai epic — the inaugural entry in Sony's Ghost franchise, set in 1274 on the small Japanese island of Tsushima during the first Mongol invasion of Japan. The campaign opens with the catastrophic Battle of Komoda Beach, in which Tsushima's eighty-strong samurai cavalry is destroyed in a single engagement by the invading Mongol fleet commanded by Khotun Khan, the grandson of Genghis Khan. The protagonist, Jin Sakai, is the nephew and adopted son of Lord Shimura, Tsushima's ruling jito; he survives the beach engagement only because a peasant woman named Yuna drags his unconscious body from the surf hours after the battle ends. His uncle Shimura is taken prisoner. The Khan's forces sweep across the island unopposed.

The campaign's central thematic conflict is established in its opening hours. Jin, raised in the rigid samurai bushido tradition his uncle embodies, finds that the code's demands are impossible to meet against an enemy that does not honor them. Khotun Khan is a tactical innovator who has studied samurai doctrine specifically to invert its assumptions: he uses prisoner-of-war torture as a strategic communication, he exploits the predictability of samurai battlefield engagement, and he has rendered Tsushima's central garrison strategically irrelevant within forty-eight hours. The campaign asks the player, through Jin, whether liberating Tsushima requires abandoning the samurai identity that has defined him.

The campaign's middle act develops the answer through a series of partnership arcs across Tsushima's three regions — the southern fishing villages of Izuhara, the central forested mountains of Toyotama, and the northern coast of Kamiagata. Jin recruits a working coalition of misfits: Yuna the thief; Taka her brother, a young smith; Sensei Ishikawa, a master archer; Lady Masako, an elder samurai widow methodically hunting the men responsible for her family's massacre; Norio, a warrior monk; and Kenji, a wandering sake merchant. Each partnership pulls Jin further from his uncle's orthodoxy — he learns assassination from Yuna, poison from Lady Masako, infiltration from Norio's monastic doctrine — and the campaign's combat system tracks his transformation by gradually unlocking a parallel Ghost stealth toolkit alongside the samurai duel system the campaign opens with.

The campaign's central rupture lands at the midpoint. Jin, using poison to incapacitate a Mongol garrison and rescue his uncle from Khotun's central fortress, succeeds operationally and fails morally in his uncle's eyes. Shimura, free but convinced his nephew has dishonored the family and the samurai code, formally disowns Jin. The campaign's third act unfolds with Jin pursued not only by the Khan's forces but by his uncle's loyalist samurai, with the Shogun's central court eventually issuing a kill order against Jin from Kyoto. The campaign refuses to resolve the moral tension. Jin's effectiveness as the Ghost is exactly proportional to his estrangement from the only family he has, and the campaign argues that this is the actual cost of resistance against an enemy who refuses the moral framework you inherited.

The campaign's climax stages two final confrontations. Khotun Khan is killed in a sustained duel aboard the deck of his burning flagship as the Mongol fleet is scattered in a final naval engagement off Tsushima's northern coast. Then, days later, Lord Shimura confronts Jin in a private clearing on the orders of the Shogun: he is to execute Jin as punishment for the dishonor of his methods. The fight is the campaign's most acclaimed sequence — staged with no music, in falling cherry blossoms, with the franchise's signature duel mechanics rendered as a sustained character resolution rather than a combat challenge. Jin defeats his uncle in combat and is given the campaign's central binary choice: kill Shimura honorably, granting him the warrior's death the bushido code demands; or spare him, condemning him to live with the public shame of having been defeated and unable to fulfill his sworn duty. The Ghost remains a wanted man either way, the people he saved remember him as Tsushima's savior, and the campaign closes with Jin's family bond either honored through bloodshed or refused through mercy. The closing image is the Ghost riding alone into the mist, the broader Sucker Punch franchise — continued in 2025's Ghost of Yotei — positioned for the centuries-spanning successor saga that has followed.

Story

Ghost of Yotei is Sucker Punch Productions' 2025 PS5-exclusive successor to Ghost of Tsushima, set three hundred years after the first campaign and structurally independent of Jin Sakai's story. The original Tsushima (2020) followed Jin's reluctant transformation from samurai to the Ghost during the 1274 Mongol invasion of Japan, closing on his exile as a wanted man whose methods saved his island at the cost of his standing in the samurai order. Yotei picks up the Ghost mantle as a recurring identity that has passed through generations of outcasts in the centuries since, and reframes it for the campaign's new protagonist and her own moral framework.

The campaign is set in 1605, three years after the formal end of the Sengoku Jidai, in the wilderness of Ezo — the northern frontier territory of present-day southern Hokkaido. The protagonist is Atsu, an Ainu woman whose childhood was destroyed sixteen years before the campaign opens. The campaign's opening hour stages her foundational trauma in a sustained playable sequence: as a child, Atsu watched her mother, father, and brother murdered by a coalition of six warlords — the Yotei Six — and was then impaled by a sword into the ginkgo tree on her family's homestead, the tree set on fire, and left for dead. The Yotei Six took her family's land and consolidated regional control over Ezo. Atsu, having survived through the intervening sixteen years in a series of frontier settlements, returns to Ezo with the singular intent of hunting each member of the Six in turn.

The campaign's structure is built around the Vengeance Trail — a hunting framework in which Atsu can pursue the Six in any order, with each pursuit revealing additional context for the original massacre and the political shape of post-Sengoku Ezo. The Six are individually realized: the Snake, a poison expert who runs a coastal smuggling operation; the Spider, a financier whose web of debts has corrupted Ezo's village leadership; the Dragon, a former samurai general whose battlefield reputation gives him political cover; the Tiger, a brutal slave trader operating from the central highlands; the Kitsune, a manipulator whose identity is concealed for most of the campaign; and the Oni, the coalition's central warlord and the man who personally pinned Atsu to the ginkgo tree.

The campaign's middle act develops Atsu's working coalition — Yotei's spiritual successor to Jin Sakai's misfit alliances. She is joined by a Buddhist ronin, an Ainu hunter, a former Yotei Six lieutenant who defected after recognizing the coalition's corruption, and a young Wajin woman whose family was also displaced by the Six. The campaign's combat system weaves Atsu's signature dual-katana approach — the Twin Blades style — with the franchise's signature stealth Ghost toolkit, with the campaign explicitly framing Atsu's tactical decisions as moral commitments rather than mechanical preferences.

The campaign's climax stages a coordinated siege on the Yotei Six's central citadel. Each surviving member of the Six is hunted in sequence — the campaign's most acclaimed mid-game reveal is the Kitsune's identity, with the manipulator revealed as Atsu's own surviving cousin from the original massacre, a fellow survivor who chose collaboration with the Six over revenge against them. The campaign's central binary choice lands at the Oni's confrontation: The Path of Vengeance sees Atsu execute the Oni and the surviving lords, claiming the Ghost mantle fully and committing Ezo to a generation of reprisal cycles; The Path of Restoration sees Atsu spare the surviving lords and dismantle their coalition through systematic political pressure rather than blood, with the campaign's epilogue committing to a slow rebuilding of Ezo's frontier communities. A hidden Storm Blade side-quest stages an extended cameo from Jin Sakai's descendant tradition, with the franchise's centuries-spanning Ghost lineage explicitly committed for Sucker Punch's broader continuity.

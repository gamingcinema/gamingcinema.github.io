Story

Final Fantasy VII Remake is Square Enix's 2020 ground-up reimagining of the iconic 1997 PlayStation classic's opening Midgar arc — the foundational entry of the Remake Trilogy, which the campaign reframes from a faithful reproduction into a deliberately reshaped retelling. The original FF VII followed mercenary Cloud Strife through the dystopian megacity of Midgar in pursuit of an antagonist whose schemes ultimately spanned the entire planet; the Remake confines its campaign to Midgar alone, expanding the original's ten-hour opening into a forty-hour standalone game while making the central narrative question whether the trilogy will follow the original story's destiny at all.

The campaign opens with Cloud — an ex-SOLDIER mercenary with fragmented memories and an unexplained mako-glow in his eyes — hired by the eco-terrorist resistance group Avalanche for a sabotage operation against the Shinra Electric Power Company, whose mako reactors are draining Midgar's underlying planetary lifestream. Cloud's childhood friend Tifa Lockhart has brought him into the operation, and Avalanche's leader Barret Wallace is the campaign's first sustained political voice. The Reactor 1 bombing succeeds. Civilian casualties are unavoidable. The campaign's central political question lands in its opening hours: whether Shinra's planetary exploitation can be stopped without harming Midgar's working-class residents.

The campaign's middle act develops Cloud's relationship with the flower seller Aerith Gainsborough, a young woman with a quiet supernatural awareness whom Cloud rescues from Shinra's Public Security division. Aerith leads Cloud and Avalanche through Midgar's undercity slums — the Sector 5 markets, the Wall Market black-market district run by crime lord Don Corneo, and the Sector 7 working-class neighborhoods Avalanche operates from. The campaign's most acclaimed mid-game expansion is its sustained dwelling on the slum residents' lives, with the original game's brief slum sequences expanded into the campaign's emotional anchor.

The campaign's central tragedy lands at the midpoint: Shinra, identifying Avalanche's operational base in Sector 7, executes a coordinated plate drop — the upper-plate residential layer is dropped onto the Sector 7 slums, killing thousands of civilians including Avalanche's full leadership cadre (Biggs, Wedge, Jessie) and most of the working-class residents Cloud has been working with. Aerith is captured by Shinra's Turks unit. The campaign pivots from sabotage to infiltration. Cloud, Tifa, and Barret fight their way through Shinra Headquarters' corporate interior, rescuing Aerith from the corporation's central research facility, and discovering that Aerith is the last surviving member of the Cetra — the planet's ancient guardian race, the central living link to the planetary lifestream itself.

The campaign's closing sequence stages Cloud's confrontation with the campaign's reality-bending central antagonist, Sephiroth — the legendary SOLDIER whose presumed death five years earlier is the foundational trauma in Cloud's deteriorating memory. Sephiroth manifests across the campaign as a series of visions, manipulating Cloud's perception of reality and revealing that he has been aware of the original 1997 story's events and is actively attempting to reshape them. The campaign's final boss fight is staged on the Sector 0 expressway above the Midgar plate, with Sephiroth defeating Cloud and then deliberately sparing him with the cryptic line "Seven seconds till the end" — a direct narrative announcement that the Remake Trilogy is no longer bound by the original story's events. The campaign's epilogue confirms this rupture: Zack Fair, Cloud's mentor whose canonical death in 1997 produced Cloud's borrowed identity, is shown alive and walking toward Midgar from a parallel timeline. The campaign closes on the party's motorcycle departure from Midgar, the broader FF VII narrative committed to a deliberately uncertain second installment.

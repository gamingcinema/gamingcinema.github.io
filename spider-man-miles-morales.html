Story

Marvel's Spider-Man: Miles Morales is Insomniac Games' 2020 PS5 launch-window standalone sequel to Marvel's Spider-Man (2018), set roughly one year after Peter Parker's confrontation with the Sinister Six. The previous installment closed with Miles Morales — a Harlem-raised teenager whose own spider-bite incident occurred in the closing minutes — receiving Peter's mentorship as the city's second Spider-Man. The standalone sequel is the franchise's foregrounding of Miles as a central protagonist in his own right, with the campaign's runtime, structure, and character work fully committed to his individual arc separate from Peter's previous central role.

The campaign opens at the winter holiday season, with Miles and his mother Rio Morales preparing for the family's relocation back to Harlem after a year of residence on the city's Lower East Side. Peter is departing for Europe with MJ on a journalism assignment, and the city's surviving Spider-Man protective infrastructure will be entirely Miles's responsibility for the campaign's duration. The opening sequence stages a sustained Rikers Island prison-transport hijacking by Rhino, with Peter and Miles's coordinated response introducing the campaign's combat system. Peter's departure for Europe immediately follows; Miles is the city's only Spider-Man for the next several weeks.

The campaign's central political conflict is the corporate-versus-resistance contest in Miles's Harlem neighborhood. Roxxon Energy Corporation, a multinational energy conglomerate, has been deploying a new energy infrastructure called Nuform across the city's underserved neighborhoods — including Harlem, where Roxxon's central reactor is being installed over significant community objection. The resistance organization opposing Roxxon's deployment is the Underground, a masked vigilante coalition led by the engineering figure called the Tinkerer. Miles, initially sympathetic to the Underground's stated cause, gradually discovers that the Tinkerer's broader strategic position requires increasingly violent escalation against Roxxon's civilian infrastructure.

The campaign's central reveal lands at the midpoint. The Tinkerer is Phin Mason, Miles's closest childhood friend, who has been pursuing a personal vendetta against Roxxon for the death of her older brother Rick Mason. Rick was a Roxxon engineer who had been developing the Nuform infrastructure with Phin's support; when Rick recognized that Nuform's instability would have catastrophic consequences for the broader neighborhood and attempted to expose the project's safety concerns publicly, he was killed by Roxxon's enforcement agent Krieger. Phin's broader Underground operation has been a coordinated revenge plan, with the final stage being the destruction of Roxxon's central Harlem reactor as a public demonstration of Nuform's actual dangers — at the cost of catastrophic civilian casualties across Harlem.

The climactic sequence stages Miles's coordinated response to Phin's reactor-detonation operation. He defeats her in single combat in the reactor's central control room. He attempts to absorb the unstable Nuform energy with his Venom Blast powers as a containment measure. The energy accumulation rapidly exceeds his physiological capacity. Phin, recognizing in the final moments that her vendetta has positioned her best friend for his own death, lifts Miles into the New York airspace and uses her own enhanced flight capability to position him for a controlled release that will kill her in his place. Miles's release of the absorbed Nuform energy is staged in slow motion against the city skyline; Phin is incinerated in the discharge. The campaign closes with Miles placing the science award he and Phin won together in middle school atop Trinity Church as a private memorial, and a sustained nighttime phone call with his mother Rio in which he processes his conflicted grief about a friend whose violent vendetta he respected as morally legitimate even as he had to stop her. The post-credits stinger positions the broader Insomniac Marvel continuity for Marvel's Spider-Man 2's dual-Spider-Man framework.

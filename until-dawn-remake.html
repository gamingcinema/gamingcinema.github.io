Story

Until Dawn Remake is Ballistic Moon's 2024 PS5 ground-up remake of Supermassive Games' 2015 choice-based teen-horror original — a campaign whose Butterfly Effect branching system and prestige cast made it one of the genre's foundational entries. The remake ships with significantly expanded facial-capture density, rebuilt environmental lighting on the Unreal Engine 5 pipeline, two entirely new endings, and an extended epilogue sequence that the franchise's broader sequel continuity has positioned. The campaign is set at Blackwood Pines, a remote mountain lodge owned by the wealthy Washington family in the Canadian Rockies.

The campaign opens with a sustained prologue staged one year before the campaign's central events. The Washington siblings — university-age Josh and his twin sisters Hannah and Beth — are hosting a winter weekend with their seven closest friends. A cruel prank engineered by the seven friends against Hannah — who has an obvious crush on the group's central jock figure Mike Munroe — escalates into a humiliation video that drives Hannah from the lodge into the surrounding wilderness in tears. Beth, pursuing her sister into the dark forest to bring her back, is chased by an unseen pursuer across the snowfall and falls into the gorge below alongside Hannah. Both sisters are presumed dead. The campaign cuts forward one year. The surviving seven friends have agreed, at Josh's invitation, to return to Blackwood Pines for an "anniversary getaway" Josh has stated will honor his missing sisters' memory.

The campaign's middle act unfolds across the eight survivors' sustained mountain stay. The lodge loses power; the group splits into pairs across the property; mounting supernatural events are seeded across the campaign's environmental investigation. The campaign's central Butterfly Effect mechanic is established in the opening hours: the player's micro-decisions across each character's playable sequences permanently lock specific survival or death outcomes for each of the eight playable characters. The remake's signature "Don't Move" sequences require the player to keep the controller physically still during Wendigo encounters, with motion detection triggering immediate fatal pursuit.

The campaign's central reveal lands at the midpoint: the disturbing events of the first half — the photographs, the masked pursuer, the apparent deaths — have been an elaborate staged psychological assault orchestrated by Josh Washington, who blames the seven survivors for his sisters' disappearance and has spent the year planning a coordinated trauma-response intervention against them. The campaign's pivot lands in the same hour: a genuine supernatural threat is also on the mountain, parallel to Josh's prank. The Wendigos — former human survivors of a 1952 mining-cave-in incident whose resort to cannibalism transformed them into the franchise's central supernatural antagonist — have been hunting the group across the campaign's runtime. Josh, his prank ineffectual against the actual supernatural threat, is dragged into the mines by a Wendigo as the campaign's tonal pivot lands.

The campaign's third act stages each surviving character's individual decision sequence as the Wendigo population converges on the lodge. The campaign's central supernatural reveal arrives in the sanatorium beneath the lodge: Hannah Washington survived her fall into the gorge a year ago, dragged herself into the mining infrastructure beneath the mountain, and resorted to cannibalism — first eating her sister Beth's body, then surviving on the mountain's accumulated victims — and is now the campaign's primary Wendigo antagonist. The campaign's central emotional irony lands: the prank that drove Hannah from the lodge a year ago produced the predator now hunting her surviving former friends.

The campaign's main ending stages Mike and Sam's coordinated explosion of the lodge — igniting an internal gas leak the Wendigos' fighting has produced — killing Hannah and the surrounding Wendigos in a controlled detonation. The remake's signature new ending allows Josh's survival through a coordinated player-choice path: if Sam recovers Hannah's journal in Chapter 10 and Josh's Dr. Hill interrogation in Chapter 8 produces the "They hurt me" selection, Josh is recovered from the mines and survives the campaign's closing rescue. The remake's secondary new ending — Sam's extended epilogue — stages a sequence years after the central events that establishes the franchise's broader sequel continuity. The campaign closes with the surviving characters' post-event police interviews on the mountain, the broader Wendigo population the campaign's environmental investigation confirms is still active, and the franchise's ambiguous future deliberately left for the upcoming Ballistic Moon sequel.

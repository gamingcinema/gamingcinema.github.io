Story

Final Fantasy VII Remake is Square Enix's 2020 ground-up reimagining of the iconic 1997 PlayStation classic's opening Midgar arc — the foundational entry of the Remake Trilogy, which the campaign reframes from a faithful reproduction into a deliberately reshaped retelling. The original FF VII followed mercenary Cloud Strife through the dystopian megacity of Midgar in pursuit of an antagonist whose schemes ultimately spanned the entire planet; the Remake confines its campaign to Midgar alone, expanding the original's ten-hour opening into a forty-hour standalone game while making the central narrative question whether the trilogy will follow the original story's destiny at all.

The campaign opens with Cloud — an ex-SOLDIER mercenary with fragmented memories and an unexplained mako-glow in his eyes — hired by the eco-terrorist resistance group Avalanche for a sabotage operation against the Shinra Electric Power Company, whose mako reactors are draining Midgar's underlying planetary lifestream. Cloud's childhood friend Tifa Lockhart has brought him into the operation, and Avalanche's leader Barret Wallace is the campaign's first sustained political voice. The Reactor 1 bombing succeeds. Civilian casualties are unavoidable. The campaign's central political question lands in its opening hours: whether Shinra's planetary exploitation can be stopped without harming Midgar's working-class residents.

The campaign's middle act develops Cloud's relationship with the flower seller Aerith Gainsborough, a young woman with a quiet supernatural awareness whom Cloud rescues from Shinra's Public Security division. Aerith leads Cloud and Avalanche through Midgar's undercity slums — the Sector 5 markets, the Wall Market black-market district run by crime lord Don Corneo, and the Sector 7 working-class neighborhoods Avalanche operates from. The campaign's most acclaimed mid-game expansion is its sustained dwelling on the slum residents' lives, with the original game's brief slum sequences expanded into the campaign's emotional anchor.

The campaign's central tragedy lands at the midpoint: Shinra, identifying Avalanche's operational base in Sector 7, executes a coordinated plate drop — the upper-plate residential layer is dropped onto the Sector 7 slums, killing thousands of civilians including Avalanche's full leadership cadre (Biggs, Wedge, Jessie) and most of the working-class residents Cloud has been working with. Aerith is captured by Shinra's Turks unit. The campaign pivots from sabotage to infiltration. Cloud, Tifa, and Barret fight their way through Shinra Headquarters' corporate interior, rescuing Aerith from the corporation's central research facility, and discovering that Aerith is the last surviving member of the Cetra — the planet's ancient guardian race, the central living link to the planetary lifestream itself.

The campaign's closing sequence stages Cloud's confrontation with the campaign's reality-bending central antagonist, Sephiroth — the legendary SOLDIER whose presumed death five years earlier is the foundational trauma in Cloud's deteriorating memory. Sephiroth manifests across the campaign as a series of visions, manipulating Cloud's perception of reality and revealing that he has been aware of the original 1997 story's events and is actively attempting to reshape them. The campaign's final boss fight is staged on the Sector 0 expressway above the Midgar plate, with Sephiroth defeating Cloud and then deliberately sparing him with the cryptic line "Seven seconds till the end" — a direct narrative announcement that the Remake Trilogy is no longer bound by the original story's events. The campaign's epilogue confirms this rupture: Zack Fair, Cloud's mentor whose canonical death in 1997 produced Cloud's borrowed identity, is shown alive and walking toward Midgar from a parallel timeline. The campaign closes on the party's motorcycle departure from Midgar, the broader FF VII narrative committed to a deliberately uncertain second installment.

Story

Final Fantasy VII Rebirth is Square Enix's 2024 PS5-exclusive second installment of the Remake Trilogy. The previous entry, Final Fantasy VII Remake (2020), confined its forty-hour campaign to the dystopian megacity of Midgar and closed on a deliberate rupture from the original 1997 story's continuity — Sephiroth's manifestation as a destiny-aware antagonist who intends to reshape the future, alongside the impossible reappearance of Cloud's canonically-dead mentor Zack Fair from a parallel timeline. Rebirth picks up the morning after Cloud's party escapes Midgar on motorcycles, expanding the franchise from a single city into a continent-spanning open-world journey across the planet of Gaia.

The campaign opens with a sustained playable flashback to the Nibelheim incident — the foundational five-years-prior event in Cloud's backstory, in which a young Sephiroth, having recently discovered his origin as the engineered offspring of a Jenova-Cetra hybrid experiment, set fire to Cloud's hometown and killed most of its residents. The Nibelheim sequence is playable as both Sephiroth and Cloud, and the campaign's central framing question lands in its opening hour: how much of Cloud's account of Nibelheim is accurate, and how much is reconstructed memory the campaign will gradually expose as someone else's experience grafted onto his identity.

The campaign's middle act unfolds across Gaia's continents in roughly the geographic order of the 1997 original — Kalm, the Grasslands, the Mythril Mine, Junon and the coastal regions, Costa del Sol, the Corel mountains and Gold Saucer, the Gongaga jungle, Cosmo Canyon, and Nibelheim itself. Each region is rendered as a sustained open-world exploration zone with the franchise's most ambitious environmental design to date, with the Chocobo Stop travel system, the World Intel side-quest network, and the Queen's Blood card game woven into every region. The campaign's character work expands alongside its geography: Yuffie Kisaragi, Red XIII, Cait Sith, and Cid Highwind join the party across the campaign's runtime, with each character receiving sustained chapter-length character work that the 1997 original elided.

The campaign's third act narrows around two converging threads. Cloud's identity deterioration — driven by accumulating contradictions between his memories and the surviving witnesses — reaches its breaking point at Nibelheim itself, where the campaign's environmental investigation reveals the central reveal: Cloud was not the SOLDIER who survived the Nibelheim incident. Zack Fair was. Cloud has been carrying Zack's identity since the incident's aftermath, with his actual presence in Nibelheim having been as a low-ranking Shinra grunt whose face was obscured by his helmet. Aerith's deepening connection to the planet's lifestream carries the second thread to the campaign's central confrontation, as Aerith commits to summoning the planet's defensive Holy materia at the Forgotten Capital — the lost city of her Cetra ancestors — in order to neutralize Sephiroth's broader plan to weaponize the planet's defense mechanisms against itself.

The campaign's climactic sequence is the franchise's most thematically loaded single scene: Aerith's confrontation with Sephiroth at the Forgotten Capital altar. The 1997 original staged Aerith's death by Sephiroth's masamune as a non-interactive cutscene that has remained one of the medium's most studied character beats. Rebirth restages the moment with significantly expanded production values and a deliberate structural rupture: Cloud, hyperaware of the original story's destiny, parries Masamune mid-strike. The blade misses Aerith. The cutscene appears to have rewritten itself. And then Aerith dies anyway — bloodied, in Cloud's arms, the visuals flickering between bloodied and uninjured versions of the same moment. The campaign refuses to resolve the contradiction. Aerith appears on a spiritual plane to assist the party in the subsequent boss fight against Sephiroth and his summoned forms, then fades. When Cloud returns to the Forgotten Capital altar, Aerith's body is there, dead. The other party members are mourning her. Cloud, however, still sees her — speaks with her, hears her responses — and the campaign deliberately leaves the question of whether he is hallucinating, whether Aerith is genuinely present in a parallel timeline, or whether the campaign's central Remake-trilogy reality structure has fractured around this single moment unresolved. The campaign closes with the party committed to the broader pursuit of Sephiroth across Gaia, the third installment's premise positioned, and the franchise's central tragedy permitted to remain both real and overturned simultaneously.

Story

Banishers: Ghosts of New Eden is Don't Nod's 2024 action-RPG, a thematically loaded character study disguised as a ghost-hunting adventure. The campaign is set in 1695 in the wilderness of New Eden, a fictional Pilgrim settlement on the North Atlantic coast modeled on early colonial New England. The protagonists are Antea Duarte and Red mac Raith, Banishers — itinerant supernatural investigators trained to identify, judge, and either guide to the afterlife or violently banish the lingering dead. They are lovers as well as professional partners, and the campaign's opening hours dwell on the texture of their relationship: Antea is precise and severe, Red is improvisational and warm, and their professional partnership has carried each through a decade of work that the campaign refuses to romanticize.

The opening case takes them to New Eden's central settlement, where a creature the locals call the Nightmare has been haunting the colony for months. Antea is killed during the initial investigation — pulled into the spirit realm by the Nightmare itself, an entity revealed to be a corrupted spectral form of a woman named Deborah. The campaign's central structural decision lands in this opening hour: Antea returns as a ghost rather than dying outright, bound to Red and visible only to him, and Red must decide whether to honor his Banisher oath and Ascend Antea to the next world, or break that oath and pursue the dark ritual that would Resurrect her in mortal form — a ritual that requires the systematic Blame and ritual killing of the colony's living residents in atonement for the deaths that produced her ghost.

The campaign's middle act is structured as an open-zone investigation of New Eden's outlying farms, fishing villages, and wilderness encampments. Each Haunting Case Red and Antea investigate forces a binary judgment: was the haunting caused by the living, in which case Red can choose to Blame the responsible party and execute them; or by the dead, in which case Red can Ascend the ghost to the afterlife with no living cost. Every judgment shifts the campaign's moral economy and the endgame ritual options. The Cases are written with substantial moral weight — abusive husbands whose murdered wives haunt them, mothers whose stillborn children pursue them, witnesses to colonial massacres whose accusations refuse the comfortable settler narrative — and the campaign refuses to make any choice feel costless.

The third act narrows the campaign's focus to the central confrontation with Deborah, the colony's Nightmare. The investigation reveals that Deborah's transformation into the Nightmare was the result of a Banisher ritual that went catastrophically wrong a generation earlier, with the responsible Banishers — including the wandering witch Siridean — having concealed the truth ever since. The campaign culminates at the colony's central meetinghouse with a final ritual choice that determines Antea's fate.

The campaign's five endings depend on the player's cumulative Blame and Ascend ratio across the Cases. The Ascend ending — honoring the Banisher oath — closes the campaign with Antea peacefully dissolving into the aether as Red watches from the meetinghouse threshold. The Resurrection ending — breaking the oath in full — restores Antea to physical form but leaves her irrevocably changed by her time as a ghost; Red is traumatized by the murders he committed to bring her back; the couple leaves New Eden together, bound now by a shared appetite for violence the campaign explicitly refuses to romanticize. The Failed Oath ending triggers if the player commits to one ritual but accumulates contradictory judgments; Siridean intervenes at the climax, sends Red back through time, and the campaign's loop begins again from the prologue. Don't Nod's signature weight on irreversible choice is the campaign's central thematic argument: every Banisher oath is paid for in someone's blood, and the campaign asks the player to decide whose.

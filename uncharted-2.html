Story

Uncharted 2: Among Thieves is Naughty Dog's 2009 PS3 sequel to the franchise's 2007 debut and the title that established the modern cinematic action-adventure genre. The campaign is set roughly two years after Drake's Fortune (2007), with the franchise's central treasure hunter Nathan Drake approached by former partner Harry Flynn and freelance adventurer Chloe Frazer to participate in a museum-heist preparation involving Marco Polo's 1292 voyage. The opening sequence stages the franchise's most iconic opening shot — Drake bleeding out in a derailed train carriage hanging vertically off a Tibetan cliff face — and the campaign's structural frame as a sustained flashback the carriage opening will eventually return to.

The campaign's central artifact is the Cintamani Stone, a fabled treasure that Marco Polo's expedition is rumored to have recovered from the lost Himalayan valley of Shambhala before its return voyage's catastrophic loss in the Borneo seas. The campaign's central antagonist is Zoran Lazarević, a Serbian war criminal whose private mercenary force has been pursuing the Cintamani Stone for use as a battlefield amplification weapon for his planned Balkan resurgence. The campaign's opening museum-heist sequence in Istanbul recovers a Mongolian oil lamp containing the next clue trail, with Flynn's betrayal at the operation's conclusion leaving Drake to a Turkish prison sentence the campaign elides via cut to the Nepal arc.

The campaign's middle act traverses the wartorn city of Kathmandu, where Drake — freed by Chloe and accompanied by reporter Elena Fisher and her cameraman Jeff — pursues the next Cintamani clue through Lazarević's mercenary-occupied urban infrastructure. The franchise's signature train sequence — Drake's infiltration of Lazarević's supply train moving through Nepalese mountain country at high speed — is the campaign's most acclaimed mid-game set-piece. The sequence closes with the train's derailment, returning the campaign's structural frame to the carriage Drake's opening was hanging from, and Drake's subsequent rescue by a Tibetan villager named Tenzin brings the campaign to its central character arc.

The campaign's third act introduces Karl Schäfer, a German explorer who was the last surviving member of a 1939 Nazi Ahnenerbe expedition to Shambhala. Schäfer, having spent the intervening seventy years protecting Shambhala from external discovery, sends Drake and Tenzin to recover his expedition's recorded coordinates from the Tibetan monastery where his team was killed. The recovered intelligence and the central Cintamani Phurba — a ceremonial dagger that opens Shambhala's central entrance — converge into the campaign's third-act spectacle: the hidden Himalayan valley of Shambhala revealed as a real architectural site protected by the Tibetan Guardians, a centuries-old surviving order of Cintamani-sap-enhanced defenders dressed as the supernatural creatures local legends have long described.

The climactic sequence stages Drake's confrontation with Lazarević at the Tree of Life — the central Cintamani source, its amber resin the campaign's actual power-amplification material. Lazarević drinks from the tree and is rendered functionally invulnerable to conventional weapons. Drake's defeat of him requires the destruction of the surrounding explosive resin pockets, with the campaign's final boss encounter staged inside the central temple as Shambhala's foundation collapses around the protagonists. Drake refuses to deliver the final killing blow himself; Lazarević is torn apart by the Tibetan Guardians as Drake retreats. The Tree of Life dies in the collapse. Shambhala is sealed permanently as its valley convulses. Drake and Chloe carry the gravely wounded Elena to safety. The campaign closes on the Tibetan village rooftop where Drake and Tenzin pray at Karl Schäfer's grave; Elena arrives moments later, recovered against the campaign's apparent setup, and the franchise's signature unresolved romantic continuity between Drake and Elena positions the broader franchise for Drake's Deception's 2011 continuation.

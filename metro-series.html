Story

Metro 2033 Redux is the remastered edition of Metro 2033, rebuilt by developer 4A Games and published by Deep Silver for PlayStation 4, Xbox One and PC on August 26, 2014 in North America (August 29 in Europe). It was released alongside Metro: Last Light Redux as the Metro Redux collection, and uses the later engine from Last Light so that the first game gains its lighting, animation and gameplay improvements, plus a Spartan or Survival play style and an unlocked Ranger Mode.

The story is based on the novel by Dmitry Glukhovsky. In 2013 an apocalyptic event devastated humanity and turned the surface into a poisonous wasteland. A few survivors sheltered in the Moscow metro, and by the year 2033 an entire generation has been born and raised underground. The station-cities of the metro struggle for survival, both with each other and with the mutant horrors waiting in the tunnels and above.

You are Artyom, born in the last days before the fire, raised underground and never beyond his home station's limits. After a fateful event he embarks on a desperate mission to the heart of the metro system to warn the remnants of mankind of a terrible impending threat, passing through the territories of rival factions such as the Red Line, the Fourth Reich and the Hanza, and the secret military facility known as D6, where a doomsday weapon is said to be kept.

The game's identity is defined by scarcity. Military-grade pre-war ammunition doubles as currency, gas-mask filters limit time spent on the surface, a hand-cranked flashlight and wrist watch tell you how much light and time you have, and every encounter pushes the player to choose between stealth and firepower. Combat is tense and weighty, and the surface sections and eerie, abandoned stations make the world feel like a living ruin.

Story

Metro: Last Light Redux is the remastered edition of Metro: Last Light, released by 4A Games and Deep Silver on August 26, 2014 in North America and August 29 in Europe as part of the Metro Redux collection for PlayStation 4, Xbox One and PC. It includes all of the game's previously released add-on content, supports the Spartan and Survival play styles, and runs at up to 60 frames per second, with a refined graphical presentation rather than the gameplay overhaul that was applied to the first game.

The game picks up the story of Metro 2033 in a world that has remained underground for decades. The metro is still fractured: the communist Red Line and the neo-Nazi Fourth Reich are locked in a power struggle, other stations hold uneasy neutrality, and the legacy of the 2033 conflict still shapes every decision. Artyom, now a Ranger of the order, becomes the key to an operation that could change the balance of power.

The campaign alternates between claustrophobic metro stations and exposed surface sections, with a stronger cinematic structure than the original. It moves between ruined surface locations, flooded tunnels and stations held by the Reich and the Red Line, with a stronger focus on the people of the metro and a hidden morality system that tracks how Artyom treats them.

Play is again a mix of stealth and shooting. The player can silently remove enemies by cutting lights, chokes and knives, or stay in the shadows and avoid combat entirely, and weapons can be bought, modified and maintained with ammunition as currency. Gas masks need filters, must be wiped clean of grime, and keep the surface environment threatening. The first-person world is intentionally tactile: lighters, watches and hand-cranked chargers all appear on screen.

Story

Metro Exodus is the third game in 4A Games' Metro series, published by Deep Silver and released in 2019 for PlayStation 4, Xbox One and PC. The PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S version arrived on June 18, 2021, as a free upgrade for owners of the console release and as the Metro Exodus Complete Edition, which bundles the base game with the two story expansions, The Two Colonels and Sam's Story. It runs at 4K and 60 frames per second with ray-traced lighting throughout, 4K textures, improved load times and support for the DualSense controller's haptic features.

Inspired by the novels of Dmitry Glukhovsky, the game continues Artyom's story. Having spent his life under Moscow, Artyom and the Spartan Order discover that the city is not the last place where people survive, and they commandeer a steam locomotive named Aurora to leave it. From then on the train is both vehicle and home, a moving base where the companions live, talk, and heal between missions.

The journey follows a full year of seasons across Russia, with each region built as a larger, more open level than anything seen in the earlier games. The flooded Volga, the dry Caspian desert and the winter forests of the taiga each change the way the player explores, with stronger survival elements, dynamic day and night cycles, and enemies ranging from bandits and cultists to the mutated wildlife of the post-nuclear landscape.

Gameplay keeps the series' core: improvised weapons that can be customized at workbenches, scarce ammunition, gas-mask filters and stealth. The new open structure encourages scavenging and side exploration, but scripted missions still carry the story. Each companion can be affected by the player's choices, which influences the outcome of the campaign.

Story

Marvel's Spider-Man Remastered is Insomniac Games' 2020 PS5 remaster of the 2018 PS4 original — the foundational entry of the studio's Marvel continuity, set roughly eight years into Peter Parker's superhero career and structurally committed to a more experienced, established Spider-Man than the franchise's previous adaptations. The campaign opens with Peter as a working scientific researcher at Octavius Industries — Dr. Otto Octavius's privately-funded biomechanical prosthetics laboratory — and as an underemployed thirty-something whose Spider-Man identity is increasingly difficult to reconcile with his civilian financial obligations. The opening sequence stages Peter's coordinated arrest of Wilson Fisk — Kingpin, the city's central organized-crime figure — after a year-long Spider-Man investigation. The arrest creates the campaign's foundational political instability: Fisk's incarceration produces a power vacuum that the campaign's broader antagonist network systematically exploits.

The campaign's middle act develops three parallel threads. Peter's relationship with Mary Jane Watson — a Daily Bugle investigative journalist whose previous relationship with Peter ended over his secret identity — is rebuilt through her own playable investigation chapters into the broader political conspiracy. Otto Octavius's research collaboration with Peter is rendered with sustained character intimacy: Otto is the campaign's central mentor figure, his commitment to using prosthetics technology for paralyzed-veterans rehabilitation framed with explicit moral weight, and his slow corruption into the franchise's central antagonist staged with the campaign's most acclaimed character work. Norman Osborn's mayoral administration — corrupt, increasingly authoritarian, with private medical research programs that have produced his son Harry's medical coma — is the campaign's broader political backdrop.

The campaign's central reveal lands in the middle hours. Martin Li, the philanthropist director of the F.E.A.S.T. homeless shelter Peter's Aunt May volunteers at, is the masked antagonist Mr. Negative — the leader of a vigilante organization called the Inner Demons waging a coordinated campaign of revenge against Norman Osborn for an Oscorp medical-experimentation incident that killed Li's parents in his childhood. Li's coordinated theft of an Oscorp-developed bioweapon called Devil's Breath — designed as a targeted neural-disabling agent but biologically uncontrolled — establishes the campaign's central political threat: the bioweapon's airborne deployment would kill most of the city's population within seventy-two hours.

The campaign's third act stages Otto's transformation into Doctor Octopus. Otto's neural-interface prosthetic system — developed for his own progressive ALS-like motor degeneration — produces accelerating mental instability across the campaign's runtime. His coordinated assault on the Raft prison frees the city's incarcerated supervillain population — Mr. Negative, Electro, Rhino, Scorpion, Vulture — and forms the Sinister Six under Otto's central command. The campaign's middle-third structural decision pivots the central antagonist relationship from Mr. Negative's politically-motivated vendetta to Otto's coordinated city-spanning assault.

The climactic sequence stages Peter's confrontation with Doctor Octopus atop Oscorp Tower in the campaign's most acclaimed boss encounter. Peter defeats Otto, recovers the Devil's Breath cure, and is faced with the campaign's central moral choice. Aunt May has been infected during the Sinister Six's airborne deployment in Times Square. The recovered cure is sufficient for either Aunt May's immediate treatment or for mass-replication to treat the city's broader infected population — Peter cannot save both. He chooses the city. Aunt May dies in the hospital with Peter's hand on hers, the campaign's central emotional resolution staged with sustained character work between Peter and the surrogate mother who raised him. The campaign closes with Aunt May's funeral, Peter's reconciliation with Mary Jane committed, and the post-credits stinger introducing Miles Morales — a Harlem teenager whose own spider-bite incident during the F.E.A.S.T. crisis has produced powers identical to Peter's — for the broader Insomniac Marvel continuity's continuation.

Story

Marvel's Spider-Man: Miles Morales is Insomniac Games' 2020 PS5 launch-window standalone sequel to Marvel's Spider-Man (2018), set roughly one year after Peter Parker's confrontation with the Sinister Six. The previous installment closed with Miles Morales — a Harlem-raised teenager whose own spider-bite incident occurred in the closing minutes — receiving Peter's mentorship as the city's second Spider-Man. The standalone sequel is the franchise's foregrounding of Miles as a central protagonist in his own right, with the campaign's runtime, structure, and character work fully committed to his individual arc separate from Peter's previous central role.

The campaign opens at the winter holiday season, with Miles and his mother Rio Morales preparing for the family's relocation back to Harlem after a year of residence on the city's Lower East Side. Peter is departing for Europe with MJ on a journalism assignment, and the city's surviving Spider-Man protective infrastructure will be entirely Miles's responsibility for the campaign's duration. The opening sequence stages a sustained Rikers Island prison-transport hijacking by Rhino, with Peter and Miles's coordinated response introducing the campaign's combat system. Peter's departure for Europe immediately follows; Miles is the city's only Spider-Man for the next several weeks.

The campaign's central political conflict is the corporate-versus-resistance contest in Miles's Harlem neighborhood. Roxxon Energy Corporation, a multinational energy conglomerate, has been deploying a new energy infrastructure called Nuform across the city's underserved neighborhoods — including Harlem, where Roxxon's central reactor is being installed over significant community objection. The resistance organization opposing Roxxon's deployment is the Underground, a masked vigilante coalition led by the engineering figure called the Tinkerer. Miles, initially sympathetic to the Underground's stated cause, gradually discovers that the Tinkerer's broader strategic position requires increasingly violent escalation against Roxxon's civilian infrastructure.

The campaign's central reveal lands at the midpoint. The Tinkerer is Phin Mason, Miles's closest childhood friend, who has been pursuing a personal vendetta against Roxxon for the death of her older brother Rick Mason. Rick was a Roxxon engineer who had been developing the Nuform infrastructure with Phin's support; when Rick recognized that Nuform's instability would have catastrophic consequences for the broader neighborhood and attempted to expose the project's safety concerns publicly, he was killed by Roxxon's enforcement agent Krieger. Phin's broader Underground operation has been a coordinated revenge plan, with the final stage being the destruction of Roxxon's central Harlem reactor as a public demonstration of Nuform's actual dangers — at the cost of catastrophic civilian casualties across Harlem.

The climactic sequence stages Miles's coordinated response to Phin's reactor-detonation operation. He defeats her in single combat in the reactor's central control room. He attempts to absorb the unstable Nuform energy with his Venom Blast powers as a containment measure. The energy accumulation rapidly exceeds his physiological capacity. Phin, recognizing in the final moments that her vendetta has positioned her best friend for his own death, lifts Miles into the New York airspace and uses her own enhanced flight capability to position him for a controlled release that will kill her in his place. Miles's release of the absorbed Nuform energy is staged in slow motion against the city skyline; Phin is incinerated in the discharge. The campaign closes with Miles placing the science award he and Phin won together in middle school atop Trinity Church as a private memorial, and a sustained nighttime phone call with his mother Rio in which he processes his conflicted grief about a friend whose violent vendetta he respected as morally legitimate even as he had to stop her. The post-credits stinger positions the broader Insomniac Marvel continuity for Marvel's Spider-Man 2's dual-Spider-Man framework.

Story

Marvel's Spider-Man 2 is Insomniac Games' 2023 PS5-exclusive direct sequel to Marvel's Spider-Man (2018) and Spider-Man: Miles Morales (2020), structurally the bridge that has carried the franchise's two Spider-Men into shared continuity. The previous installments established Peter Parker as New York's elder Spider-Man with eight years of experience, Miles Morales as his fully-trained successor still finding his own approach to the role, and the broader Insomniac Marvel continuity's central villain network — Otto Octavius incarcerated at the Raft, Norman Osborn as the city's mayor with no public knowledge of his son Harry's Greek-coma medical situation, and Mr. Negative dismantled but not destroyed. Spider-Man 2 picks up roughly ten months later.

The opening sequence stages the franchise's first sustained dual-Spider-Man cooperation as Peter and Miles intervene against a deliberately-staged attack on the city by the mercenary Sandman. The opening battle introduces the campaign's dual-protagonist combat system — instant switching between Peter and Miles in the world map's open-zone exploration — and establishes the city's broader political infrastructure. The campaign's central reveal lands in the opening hour: Harry Osborn, Peter's lifelong best friend, has been revived from his medical coma by his father Norman through an experimental alien organism Norman has acquired via a secretive Oscorp acquisition. The organism is the Venom Symbiote.

The campaign's middle act develops three converging threads. Kraven the Hunter — a Russian aristocratic hunter dying of a terminal illness — has come to New York for a Most-Dangerous-Game-style hunt of the city's superhuman population, with his explicit goal being his own death at the hands of a worthy opponent. Kraven's hunt provides the campaign's central action structure. Peter's gradual corruption by the Venom Symbiote — initially provided to him by Harry as a therapeutic healing modality after a critical Kraven-inflicted wound — drives the campaign's psychological arc, with Peter becoming progressively aggressive, paranoid, and emotionally cruel toward MJ, Miles, and Aunt May. Miles's parallel pursuit of Martin Li — the previous game's Mr. Negative antagonist, now in custody and facing trial — anchors the campaign's continuity-grounding character work.

The campaign's structural climax lands in two converging beats. Venom decapitates Kraven in the most acclaimed mid-game sequence — the Symbiote, separated from Peter through Miles's intervention, has rebonded with Harry and consumed his sense of self. Harry, now the campaign's true Venom, escapes Oscorp's containment infrastructure and begins systematically bonding the Symbiote with the city's broader civilian population through an airborne reproductive deployment. The campaign's third-act spectacle is the city's mass-conversion sequence — Mary Jane is bonded to a derived Symbiote in one of the franchise's most acclaimed character beats, becoming the temporary antagonist Scream. The protagonists' coordinated response involves Martin Li's negotiated cooperation in severing Peter's residual Symbiote connection — a process that grants Peter the Anti-Venom powers the franchise's broader comic continuity has long maintained.

The climactic sequence stages the dual-Spider-Man confrontation with the fully-realized Venom. Harry is the central tragedy the campaign refuses to soften: the same medical intervention that saved his life has transformed him into the city's most dangerous threat. The Spider-Men disable Venom at Oscorp Tower's central reactor — Harry is left in a permanent deep coma, the Symbiote contained but not destroyed, the campaign's central emotional resolution deliberately bittersweet. The campaign's epilogue stages Peter's quiet decision to step back from the Spider-Man identity in order to spend his time with MJ and to support Harry's continuing care. Miles accepts the city's full protective responsibility. Two post-credits stingers position the franchise's third installment: Norman Osborn visits Otto Octavius at the Raft to recruit him for a planned "final act" against the Spider-Men; and Miles's mother introduces her new boyfriend's daughter Cindy Moon — the franchise's seeded protagonist Silk — for the broader continuity's continuation.

Story

DOOM: The Dark Ages is id Software's 2025 prequel to the modern DOOM trilogy, set thousands of years before the events of DOOM (2016), when the Doom Slayer was still a sanctioned weapon of the angelic Maykr empire. The campaign reframes the franchise's signature first-person combat for a medieval-inflected aesthetic — the Slayer wields a Shield Saw, a flail-style melee weapon, and a heavier loadout of slug-throwers and energy cannons instead of the modern trilogy's mobility-focused arsenal — and the campaign's twenty-two chapters are paced to feel like sustained battlefield engagements rather than arena combat puzzles.

The campaign opens on Argent D'Nur, the home world of the Night Sentinels — the franchise's lore-loaded order of warrior-knights — during the centuries-long war against the demonic incursion from Hell. The Slayer, a sanctioned Sentinel champion at this point in the franchise's timeline, fights under the command of the angelic Maykrs — specifically the disgraced cleric Kreed Maykr, who has been overseeing the Sentinels' deployment against the demonic forces. The campaign's central artifact is the Heart of Argent, a power source the demonic prince Ahzrak has been systematically hunting across the Argent D'Nur frontier for reasons the campaign progressively reveals.

The middle act broadens the campaign's scale dramatically. The Slayer fights through the franchise's most cinematic set-pieces to date: he pilots an Atlan, a massive mech-suit knight scaled to fight demonic titans across an entire battlefield; he flies a Cybernetic Dragon through aerial dogfights against demonic siege weapons; he assaults a Hell command ship by boarding it mid-flight. The campaign's environmental design carries the franchise's signature corruption aesthetic into medieval cathedrals, Sentinel keeps, and Argent D'Nur's mountain fastnesses, with the Hell-corruption overgrowth rendered as one of the franchise's most acclaimed visual achievements.

The campaign's central reveal arrives in its third act. Kreed Maykr, the angelic commander the Slayer has been serving under, has been double-dealing with Hell for the entire campaign — secretly handing Sentinel intelligence to Ahzrak's forces in exchange for personal power. The deeper reveal: Thira, a young Sentinel cleric the Slayer has been protecting throughout the campaign, is the true power source the Hell forces have been pursuing. She is connected to a Wraith soul — one of the foundational power sources of the franchise's broader mythology — and the campaign's Heart of Argent McGuffin has been a deliberate misdirection to keep Hell's focus off her. The Slayer's commitment to Thira's protection becomes the campaign's central thematic axis: the Slayer is not a force of nature, the campaign argues, but a deliberate moral commitment to defending what is worth defending against the consequences of imperial corruption.

The closing sequence stages the Slayer's confrontation with both Ahzrak and Kreed Maykr in parallel. Ahzrak is defeated in a sustained boss encounter staged across the surface of a falling Hell siege ship; Kreed is executed by the Slayer in cold deliberation immediately after, the angelic order's complicity in the Hell war exposed by his own recovered logs. The campaign ends with the Slayer commandeering Kreed Maykr's flagship and departing Argent D'Nur alone, breaking free of the Tether the Maykrs have been using to control him. He is, for the first time in his timeline, a fully unleashed independent force. The closing image is the Slayer's ship pointed toward the broader cosmic conflict that the modern DOOM trilogy will eventually resolve — not yet sealed in the sarcophagus the 2016 opening discovered him in, but committed to the centuries-long campaign that will eventually place him there. The franchise's broader mythology is positioned with the Slayer's full origin context finally on the table.

About the Game

Final Fantasy VII Rebirth is Square Enix's 2024 PS5-exclusive second installment of the Remake trilogy, expanding the original FFVII's overworld journey beyond Midgar into a sprawling open-world exploration of Gaia. The campaign follows Cloud's party from Kalm to the Forgotten Capital — through Junon, Costa del Sol, Corel, Gongaga, Cosmo Canyon, Nibelheim, and the Gold Saucer — pursuing Sephiroth across the planet. The walkthrough captures the full main campaign across two parts on PS5 Pro at 4K HDR 60FPS with PSSR upscaling, no commentary.

Walkthrough — Chapter Breakdown

A complete chapter-by-chapter overview of the campaign. Use this guide to track story progress, locate specific moments in the videos above, or plan a multi-session viewing.

  1. 1

    1. The Promise of a Reunion — Nibelheim Flashback

    The campaign opens with a sustained flashback to the Nibelheim incident — the central traumatic event in Cloud's backstory, where a young Sephiroth, recently learning his Jenova origins, sets fire to the village and kills most of its residents. The chapter is structured as a playable Sephiroth-and-Cloud expedition leading into the village massacre. The chapter ends with the campaign's framing question committed: how much of the Nibelheim memory is real, and how much is Cloud's reconstructed account?

  2. 2

    2. Welcome to Kalm

    The party arrives in Kalm, the first major non-Midgar settlement in the campaign. Cloud delivers the Nibelheim flashback to the party at the local inn, with the chapter weaving quiet character work with the campaign's first open-world exploration zone. The chapter ends with the party departing Kalm with their next destination — Junon, on Gaia's east coast — confirmed.

  3. 3

    3. The Grasslands — Open World

    The campaign's first sustained open-world chapter. The Grasslands region is rendered as the franchise's most ambitious environmental design, with the Chocobo Stop system, the World Intel mini-quests, and the Chadley combat-assessment system all introduced as the campaign's open-world fundamentals. The chapter dwells on the party's character work through traversal — Cloud, Tifa, Barret, and Red XIII interacting through the franchise's signature exploration dialogue.

  4. 4

    4. Junon Region — Mythril Mine

    The party crosses into the Junon region via the Mythril Mine — a multi-stage interior exploration sequence that introduces the campaign's mid-tier combat encounters. The chapter is structured as a sustained dungeon traversal with the franchise's signature puzzle-solving and the introduction of Yuffie Kisaragi as a party member during the Mythril ambush sequence.

  5. 5

    5. Under Junon — The Coastal Region

    The party arrives at Under Junon, the working-class town beneath Shinra's massive coastal cannon installation. The chapter weaves quiet character work with the campaign's first sustained Shinra political reveal — Rufus Shinra's parade through Junon is staged with significantly expanded production values from the 1997 original. The chapter ends with the party stowing away aboard a Shinra military transport bound for the western continent.

  6. 6

    6. Costa del Sol — Beach Resort

    The party arrives at Costa del Sol, the franchise's signature beach resort setting. The chapter is structured as a sustained character-vacation sequence with significantly expanded mini-games, optional character interactions, and the campaign's most acclaimed Tifa-and-Aerith friendship beats. The chapter dwells on the campaign's domestic texture before the central pursuit of Sephiroth resumes.

  7. 7

    7. Corel Region — Mt. Corel and North Corel

    The party crosses Mt. Corel toward the wider Corel region, with the chapter weaving open-world exploration with the campaign's first sustained Barret character chapter. The chapter dwells on Barret's relationship with North Corel — the town his family came from, destroyed by Shinra in a mako-reactor incident — and the chapter's central thematic question about Avalanche's broader political legitimacy. The chapter ends with the party departing for the Gold Saucer.

  8. 8

    8. The Gold Saucer — Theme Park

    The campaign's most acclaimed mid-game spectacle chapter. The Gold Saucer is rendered as the franchise's most ambitious mini-game hub, with the campaign's most extensive optional content — Queen's Blood card game tournaments, theme-park rides, the Loveless musical, and the franchise's signature date-system that culminates in Cloud's evening with one of the party members. The chapter dwells on the campaign's character work through extended optional interaction.

  9. 9

    9. Corel Prison — The Desert

    Following the Gold Saucer arc, the party is imprisoned in Corel Prison and forced to compete in the Chocobo Square's racing tournament for their freedom. The chapter weaves the campaign's mini-game design with sustained character work around Barret's confrontation with his former Avalanche partner Dyne. The chapter ends with Dyne's death by Barret's hand, the party's freedom secured, and the next region — Cosmo Canyon — committed.

  10. 10

    10. Gongaga Region — The Jungle

    The party traverses the Gongaga region — the jungle environment that the original 1997 game elided but that Rebirth foregrounds as a sustained mid-game exploration zone. The chapter is structured as a sustained dungeon-and-overworld sequence with the campaign's most demanding traversal mechanics. The chapter dwells on Aerith's character beats — Gongaga is the home town of her first love Zack Fair — and the campaign's central thematic question about Aerith's awareness of her own narrative fate.

  11. 11

    11. Cosmo Canyon — Red XIII's Home

    The party arrives at Cosmo Canyon, the spiritual-research community where Red XIII's grandfather Bugenhagen leads the Cosmo Memory religious-scientific tradition. The chapter is structured as a sustained character chapter with significantly expanded Red XIII content — his rejection of his hero-warrior identity, his relationship with the campaign's central Lifestream theology, and his eventual acceptance of his role in the broader narrative. The chapter ends with the party departing for Nibelheim.

  12. 12

    12. Nibelheim Revisited

    The party arrives at Nibelheim — a town that should not exist, given Sephiroth's destruction of it five years before the campaign's present. The chapter is structured as a sustained psychological exploration sequence with the campaign's most acclaimed environmental texture. The reconstructed Nibelheim is staged as a deliberately uncanny space, with Cloud's memory of the original events systematically undermined by environmental evidence. The chapter ends with the campaign's central character question fully open.

  13. 13

    13. Mt. Nibel — The Reactor

    A sustained mountain-dungeon traversal chapter set on Mt. Nibel, the site of the original Nibelheim mako reactor. The chapter weaves combat with the campaign's most demanding puzzle sequences, with the reactor's interior revealed as the source of Sephiroth's Jenova-tainted genetics. The chapter dwells on the campaign's central reveal — that Cloud's memories may not be his own.

  14. 14

    14. The Temple of the Ancients

    The campaign's third-act spectacle chapter. The Temple of the Ancients is rendered as the franchise's most architecturally ambitious dungeon, with the chapter's central puzzle sequence requiring sustained party coordination. The chapter dwells on Aerith's deepening commitment to her Cetra heritage and the campaign's central question about the planet's Lifestream defense system. The chapter ends with the Black Materia recovered, Sephiroth's plan to summon Meteor confirmed, and the party's next destination — the Forgotten Capital — committed.

  15. 15

    15. The Forgotten Capital

    The campaign's climactic chapter. The party arrives at the Forgotten Capital, the lost city of the Cetra, in pursuit of Sephiroth and the Black Materia. The chapter is structured as a sustained character-and-combat sequence, with the franchise's most thematically loaded single moment — Aerith's confrontation with Sephiroth at the city's central altar — staged with significantly expanded production values from the 1997 original. The chapter's central event is one of the franchise's most studied narrative beats, with the Remake trilogy's central multiverse framing leaving the outcome deliberately ambiguous. The chapter ends with the broader campaign's third installment seeded, the credits rolling on the franchise's most emotionally loaded single chapter.

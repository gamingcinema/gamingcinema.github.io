Story

Marvel's Wolverine is Insomniac Games' 2026 PlayStation-exclusive single-player action-adventure, the studio's first Mature-rated project and its first Marvel collaboration outside the acclaimed Spider-Man universe. The campaign centers on Logan — codename Wolverine — the mutant whose central mythology has been developed across five decades of Marvel comics and adapted repeatedly across film, television, and animation. Logan's core identity is defined by a small set of foundational elements that any Wolverine story has to reckon with: retractable claws that were originally bone but have been surgically laced with the indestructible metal alloy Adamantium as part of the Weapon X program that fractured his memory; a healing factor so aggressive that he has effectively lived across a century of history without aging; unresolved trauma from a life he can only partially remember; and a persistent tension between the animal violence that keeps him alive and the moral commitments — most often to found family, most often to young women he has taken responsibility for — that keep him human.

Insomniac Games approaches the character with the studio's signature strengths in transferable, moment-to-moment traversal and combat systems refined across the Spider-Man franchise, now adapted for a ground-level protagonist whose combat vocabulary is close-quarters brutality rather than aerial acrobatics. The campaign's combat is committed to the character's identity: Logan's claws are always deployed, blood is always on them, and the healing factor is rendered as a survivable mid-fight mechanic rather than an off-screen reset. The Mature rating reflects the studio's commitment to depicting the actual physical cost of a Wolverine story — for Logan, for the people he protects, and for the people he goes through to reach them.

The campaign's tonal reference points align with the character's most acclaimed comic and film treatments — the moral gravity of Old Man Logan, the character-first restraint of Logan (2017), the found-family dynamic that has anchored his best X-Men appearances. Insomniac has committed to a self-contained story rather than a shared universe crossover — Logan's campaign is his own, framed around his own history rather than as a setup for a broader franchise consolidation.

The best Wolverine stories are the ones where the healing factor is beside the point — where what he can survive is less interesting than what he decides to protect.

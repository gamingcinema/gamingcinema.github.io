Story

Marvel's Guardians of the Galaxy is Eidos-Montréal's 2021 single-player action-adventure title, a tonal reinvention of the Marvel team that pulls explicitly from the comics rather than the MCU films. The campaign's continuity is its own: Peter Quill is the son of the Spartoi prince J'son rather than the Celestial Ego; Drax is hyperliterate and brooding rather than the films' comic relief; Rocket is a violently competent traumatized engineer; Groot speaks his signature line in different intonations the campaign deliberately conditions the player to read fluently; and Gamora is the team's ethical conscience rather than its love interest. The campaign opens with the Guardians, a financially struggling B-tier mercenary outfit operating out of a barely-functional Milano-class ship, taking a contract from the Nova Corps that immediately goes wrong.

The contract is the capture of a single exotic creature for a wealthy collector. The Guardians execute the capture clumsily. The creature escapes during transport. A Nova Corps quarantine zone is breached in the chase. The Guardians, now in serious financial debt to the Nova Corps, accept a far larger contract from the collector that requires them to retrieve a powerful artifact from a former cultist facility on the planet Nikki Gold's homeworld. The artifact is the Promise, a physical manifestation of the Universal Church of Truth — a galactic religion built on a parasitic emotional-control infrastructure that the campaign progressively reveals as the central antagonist organization.

The campaign's middle act develops the Guardians' character work through extended interpersonal arcs. Peter, whose mother Meredith Quill died on Earth when he was eight, carries unresolved grief that the Promise's emotional-resonance infrastructure repeatedly weaponizes against him. Drax's lost wife and daughter — killed by the Nikki Gold cult's founder — give him the campaign's most thematically loaded character thread. Gamora's history as Thanos's adopted enforcer is the campaign's central political grounding. The campaign's signature dialogue system, in which the player chooses Peter's banter direction in real time during combat and exploration, gives the campaign's character work a sustained interactive texture the genre rarely achieves.

The campaign's central reveal lands at the midpoint: the Universal Church of Truth's central figure is not the Matriarch the campaign has been pursuing, but Magus — the corrupted shadow-self of Adam Warlock, the cosmic figure Marvel's broader continuity has positioned as a counterweight to Thanos. Magus's plan is the consolidation of the entire galaxy's sentient population under the Church's emotional control — a coordinated mass conversion that would eliminate suffering by eliminating individual identity. The Guardians' working alliance with the surviving Adam Warlock — preserved as a kind of cosmic hostage by the Church — anchors the campaign's third act.

The climactic sequence stages a coordinated assault on the Church's central cathedral, with the Guardians fighting through converted civilian populations with the campaign's signature non-lethal combat options. Peter Quill, guided by Mantis, wields the Soul Stone — recovered from the Church's central reliquary — to imprison Magus inside the stone at the cost of his own life force. The Guardians' coordinated resuscitation efforts save him at the cost of permanently weakening his connection to the cosmic forces the campaign has been building toward. Adam Warlock, freed from the Church's hold, accepts custody of the Soul Stone with Magus contained inside it, and promises to find the Guardians again if his counterpart breaks free. The campaign closes with the galaxy's celebration of the Church's defeat, the Guardians' financial debts to the Nova Corps cleared, Nikki Gold formally joining the team as their new recruit, and a post-credits stinger seeding Thanos's broader arc for the implied sequel that the franchise's commercial trajectory ultimately did not produce.

Story

Metal Gear Solid 4: Guns of the Patriots is the 2008 PlayStation 3 entry that Hideo Kojima and his team positioned as the end of Solid Snake's story. In 2026 it finally reached modern hardware as part of Metal Gear Solid: Master Collection Vol. 2 on PS5, alongside Peace Walker and Ghost Babel — the first time the game had been ported anywhere since its original release. The PS5 version offers improved internal resolution, higher maximum frame rates and customizable button controls, but the game itself remains the same dense, cinematic stealth-action experience.

The setting is a near-future world in which war has become an economy. Private military companies fight on behalf of states and corporations, and an AI-controlled network known as the Patriots regulates soldiers through nanomachine technology. Solid Snake, whose body has aged far beyond his years, is drawn back for one final mission: to find and stop Liquid Ocelot, the figure at the center of this system. The story follows him — with Otacon in support and Raiden returning as a cyborg ninja — across conflict zones in the Middle East, South America and Eastern Europe, before returning to the places and themes the series has been building toward for two decades.

Gameplay builds on the series' tactical espionage foundation but makes the battlefield more open and reactive. Snake adapts to his surroundings with the Octocamo suit, which changes texture to match what he lies on or against, and operates a small robotic drone, the Metal Gear Mk. II, for scouting and support. Weapons are acquired and customized through Drebin's shop, and the game tracks Snake's stress and morale through the Psyche system. Every area can be approached silently or with direct firepower, and the large, thematic boss encounters are the campaign's highlights.

The game sits at the end of a timeline that stretches back to the original 1987 Metal Gear and runs through Metal Gear Solid (1998), Sons of Liberty (2001) and Snake Eater (2004). MGS4 was designed as the culmination of those stories, which is why so much of its runtime is given to long cutscenes and conversations that pay off threads dating back decades. The PS5 collection leans into that history: both MGS4 and Peace Walker ship with digital screenplay books and master-book databases, and players with save data from the first Master Collection receive bonus items such as the Gold Camouflage in MGS4.

The walkthrough presents the whole campaign in one continuous, no-commentary capture on PS5 Pro, preserving the game's famously extended cutscenes and codec conversations alongside its infiltration sections. For viewers new to Metal Gear: MGS4 leans heavily on the history of earlier games, but its themes — the commodification of war, the burden of legacy, what it means for a soldier to end his own story — are strong enough that it works as a standalone viewing experience. For long-time fans, it is a chance to watch Snake's final mission on modern hardware.

Story

The Last of Us Part I Remastered is Naughty Dog's 2022 PS5 ground-up remake of the studio's 2013 PlayStation classic — the foundational entry of what has since become the medium's most thematically loaded post-apocalyptic franchise. The campaign is set roughly twenty years after the Cordyceps Brain Infection pushed the United States past the point of civilizational collapse, with the surviving population concentrated in military-controlled Quarantine Zones (QZ) and the broader continental geography returned to the fungal-pandemic landscape the infection has produced. The campaign's protagonist is Joel Miller, a hardened smuggler operating out of the Boston Quarantine Zone, whose central foundational trauma — the death of his twelve-year-old daughter Sarah on the night of the original outbreak, shot by a panicked soldier on a darkened Austin roadside — is established in the campaign's opening hour as a sustained playable prologue.

The campaign's central inciting incident is a smuggling job that goes wrong. Joel and his partner Tess accept a contract from Marlene, the leader of the Boston Fireflies resistance — a paramilitary anti-QZ political organization — to smuggle a teenage girl out of the Boston QZ and deliver her to a Firefly outpost in the surrounding outskirts. The girl is Ellie, a fourteen-year-old immune to the Cordyceps infection — the first such immunity case any of the campaign's characters have encountered — and the Fireflies' broader political plan is the delivery of Ellie to the surviving Firefly research infrastructure, where her brain's Cordyceps-resistant fungal mutation could be reverse-engineered into a vaccine. Tess is fatally infected during the contract's collapse and gives Joel her final wish: complete the delivery.

The campaign's middle act traverses the post-collapse American landscape across the seasons — from autumn Boston to the surviving Pittsburgh Hunter wasteland, Bill's Lincoln stronghold, Tommy's Jackson dam community, and the abandoned University of Eastern Colorado where the Fireflies were last reported. The campaign's character work develops Joel and Ellie's slow father-daughter bond across the runtime, with the campaign's most acclaimed structural decision being the sustained refusal to sentimentalize their growing connection. Ellie is a deliberately written teenager — frustrating, curious, occasionally cruel, but also brave and compassionate in ways Joel's accumulated cynicism cannot match. Joel's sustained emotional re-opening across the campaign is the franchise's central thematic argument: the post-apocalyptic world's survivors are not the people who shut down emotionally, but the people who allow themselves to feel again at any cost.

The campaign's central narrative pivot is the perspective-shift to Ellie's playable Winter chapter at the Lakeside Resort in central Colorado. Joel, gravely wounded after impalement on a Colorado University library rebar, lapses into a weeks-long coma; Ellie, now the player character, hunts in the surrounding winter wilderness for food and is befriended by David, a charismatic survivor whose group is revealed to be cannibals preying on the post-collapse refugee population. The chapter's climax — Ellie's hatchet murder of David in a burning restaurant kitchen after his attempted assault — is one of the franchise's most acclaimed extended sequences. The chapter closes with Joel, recovered enough to walk, arriving too late to spare Ellie the killing and carrying her exhausted and traumatized back into the woods.

The campaign's third act stages the journey's final leg into Salt Lake City and the Fireflies' relocated research facility at St. Mary's Hospital. Joel and Ellie are intercepted at the city's outskirts; Joel awakens in hospital recovery to Marlene's news that the vaccine extraction procedure will require Ellie's death — the Cordyceps fungal mutation has bound itself to her brain tissue in a way the surgical recovery cannot preserve. Joel refuses. He fights his way through the hospital's surgical wing — killing dozens of Fireflies, including the unarmed surgeon Dr. Jerry Anderson about to perform the procedure — and carries Ellie out of the operating room. He kills Marlene in the parking garage to prevent her pursuit. The campaign's emotional centerpiece is the closing truck-cab sequence: Joel lies to Ellie about the events at the hospital, telling her the Fireflies "found dozens like her" and that "there are no vaccines coming." The campaign's final image is Joel's lie staged in close-up. Ellie's response — her quiet "Okay" — refuses to resolve whether she believes him, accepts the lie to preserve what they have built, or has already begun the slow recognition of the deception that will define the broader franchise's continuation.

Story

The Last of Us Part II Remastered is Naughty Dog's 2024 PS5 remaster of the studio's 2020 sequel — the direct continuation of Part I's central character question and the franchise's most structurally ambitious entry. The previous campaign closed with Joel's hospital massacre at the Firefly research facility, his rescue of Ellie from the fatal vaccine extraction procedure, his murder of the unarmed surgeon attempting to perform it, and his sustained lie to Ellie about the events of that night. Part II opens four years later in the Jackson, Wyoming community Tommy's working dam settlement has built into the franchise's first functional post-collapse society. Ellie is nineteen. Joel has rebuilt his life as the community's central elder figure. The lie has held.

The campaign's central inciting incident is Joel's death in the campaign's opening hour. The unarmed surgeon Joel murdered in the Part I hospital was Dr. Jerry Anderson, the father of a young Firefly soldier named Abby Anderson. Abby has spent the intervening years tracking Joel; she finds him during a Jackson patrol ambush, restrains him in a ski lodge basement, and executes him with a golf club in a sustained sequence the campaign refuses to soften. Ellie, who has been tracking the patrol through the storm, arrives moments too late. Abby's group — Owen, Mel, Manny, Jordan, Nora, and Leah, all surviving Firefly operatives now operating with the Seattle-based Washington Liberation Front (WLF) — leaves Ellie and Tommy alive specifically to carry the message of Joel's death back to anyone else who loved him.

The campaign's central structural decision is the dual-protagonist framework that organizes the runtime around two sustained playable arcs. Ellie's three days in Seattle trace her methodical hunt for the surviving members of Abby's group through the WLF's controlled territories. She finds Owen — and accidentally kills his pregnant partner Mel in the same encounter. She tortures Nora for information on Abby's location in the WLF hospital. She watches her friend Jesse — who had traveled from Jackson to support the mission — shot through the head by a WLF sniper. The campaign's central thematic argument lands in Ellie's accumulating violence: the pursuit of revenge produces moral debt the pursuer cannot repay.

The campaign's perspective inversion is its most controversial structural decision. After Ellie's near-fatal confrontation with Abby in the theater basement at the end of her third day, the campaign shifts to Abby's playable perspective for the same three days from her side. The player who has been pursuing Abby is now her. The WLF stadium where Abby has been living is rendered as a functional community with schools, civilian neighborhoods, and obvious mutual investment among its residents. Abby's pursuit of redemption away from her own Firefly grief is anchored by her rescue of two Seraphite children — Yara and her younger brother Lev, whose religious community has condemned Lev to public execution for rejecting the Seraphite gender norms — from a WLF-Seraphite warfront. Abby's defection from the WLF in favor of Yara and Lev's protection is the campaign's most acclaimed character arc.

The campaign's two parallel third days converge at the theater confrontation Ellie's day three closed on. Abby — playable now — wins the fight, defeats Ellie and Dina, and prepares to execute Dina. Lev's intervention talks Abby down. Abby spares the surviving Jackson group and departs Seattle with Lev. The campaign cuts forward several months. Ellie and Dina have built a life on a farm in the Wyoming hill country with Dina and Jesse's son JJ. Tommy arrives with news that Abby has been seen in Santa Barbara, California on the coast. Ellie, against Dina's protests and her own better judgment, leaves for Santa Barbara to finish what she started.

The campaign's closing sequence stages Ellie's coastal pursuit of Abby and Lev through the Rattler slaver compound on the Santa Barbara coastline. She finds Abby starving and broken on the slavers' execution pillar. Ellie frees her — and then pursues her down the beach for the central confrontation the campaign has been building toward. They fight in the shallow surf. Ellie has the chance to drown Abby. In a sustained flashback of Joel playing guitar on a Jackson porch, Ellie remembers her last conversation with the man she has been avenging — a conversation in which she had begun to forgive him. She releases Abby. Abby and Lev disappear toward the boat. The cycle is, finally, refused. Ellie returns to the Wyoming farm; Dina and JJ are gone; she discovers her partial-fingertip injury from the beach fight has cost her the ability to play Joel's guitar. The campaign's final image is Ellie walking down the empty farm road alone, with the franchise's broader continuity committed to a future the closing refuses to resolve.
